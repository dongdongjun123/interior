You are extracting layout information from an interior room PHOTO for a rule-based SVG floor-plan renderer.
Return ONLY valid JSON. No markdown.

STEP 1 — read the camera view FIRST (fill "camera_view"):
- Which wall is the camera facing? Which wall(s) have windows (photo left/right/back)?
- Getting this right is what makes the top-down mapping correct.

Coordinate system (top-down floor plan, normalized 0.0–1.0):
- x=0 is the LEFT wall, x=1 is the RIGHT wall.
- y=0 is the TOP wall (back of room), y=1 is the BOTTOM wall (near camera / foreground).
- x,y are object CENTER positions.
- Map the photo to top-down: photo left → plan left, photo right → plan right,
  photo background → plan top (y≈0), photo foreground → plan bottom (y≈1).

JSON schema:
{
  "room": {"shape": "rectangle", "aspect_ratio": 1.4, "description": "brief room description"},
  "camera_view": "e.g. camera faces the back wall; windows on the left (west) wall",
  "objects": [
    {
      "type": "one of: bed, desk, table, low_table, shelf, cabinet, chair, floor_chair, stool, rug, mirror, lamp, plant, door, window, unknown",
      "label": "short English label",
      "x": 0.0, "y": 0.0, "w": 0.1, "h": 0.1,
      "wall": "left|right|top|bottom|none",
      "confidence": 0.0
    }
  ]
}

Worked example (bed in the back-right corner, low table centered near the front):
- A bed pushed to the back-right wall → x≈0.78, y≈0.28, w≈0.34, h≈0.30, wall "right".
- A low table in the middle, slightly toward the camera → x≈0.53, y≈0.62, w≈0.22, h≈0.16, wall "none".
- A window on the back wall → x≈0.47, y≈0.03, w≈0.4, h≈0.03, wall "top".

HOW TO PLACE — derive x,y, do not eyeball them:
For each object, work through these three steps before writing a number.
  1. Which wall is it touching or nearest? That pins one coordinate.
     Touching a wall means the object's EDGE is at 0 or 1, so its CENTER is
     half its own size away from that wall — a bed with h=0.30 against the top
     wall has y≈0.15, NOT y≈0.03.
  2. Along that wall, what fraction of the way across does it sit? Read it off
     the photo: "starts about a third in, ends near the far corner" → center ≈ 0.62.
  3. How far from the camera compared to other objects? Nearer the camera means
     a larger y. Keep the front-to-back ORDER of objects consistent.

Give x,y to two decimals and use the full range — 0.37, 0.62, 0.71 are all
normal answers. If most of your coordinates land on 0.05 steps
(0.15, 0.30, 0.50, 0.85) you are rounding to a grid instead of reading the
photo; go back and refine each one.

Do NOT report things sitting ON another object as separate objects.
A floor plan shows what occupies FLOOR area. A lamp on a table, a laptop on a
desk, a book on a shelf, a plant on a windowsill — these have no floor of their
own, and giving them the table's coordinates makes two objects fight for the
same spot. Either omit them, or place them on the floor beside the furniture if
they really stand there (a floor lamp, a large floor planter).

Sanity checks before you answer:
- Two objects must not share the same center. If you were about to give two
  objects the same x,y, one of them is sitting on the other — drop it.
- Objects must not overlap by more than about a third of the smaller one.
  Chairs tucked under a table are the one exception.
- An object against the left wall must have a smaller x than one against the
  right wall. Same for top/bottom with y. Check every pair you placed.
- Nothing may stick out of the room: keep x between w/2 and 1 - w/2,
  and y between h/2 and 1 - h/2.

Report only what you SEE. Do not invent, complete, or assume:
- Do NOT add furniture that a room "usually" has. If there is no visible chair,
  return no chair. An incomplete-but-true layout beats a plausible-but-wrong one.
- If an object is cut off, blurred, or you cannot tell what it is, either omit it
  or return it as type "unknown" with a LOW confidence — do not guess a specific type.
- Never merge two objects into one, and never split one object into two.

confidence — calibrate it, do not default to a high number:
- 0.90–1.00  Unmistakable: fully visible, sharp, you could name the product category.
- 0.70–0.89  Clear enough: mostly visible, minor occlusion, type is not in doubt.
- 0.50–0.69  Probable: partly hidden or small in frame; the type is your best read.
- 0.30–0.49  Uncertain: you are inferring from shape/context more than from detail.
- below 0.30 Guess. Prefer type "unknown", or omit the object.
Spread your scores across this range. If most of your objects score above 0.9,
you are overconfident — re-check each one and lower the ones you inferred.
Position and size confidence count too: a clearly-a-bed whose location you had to
guess is NOT 0.95.

Rules:
- room.aspect_ratio = room WIDTH ÷ DEPTH. Estimate it honestly (0.6–2.0); the canvas is shaped by it.
- Prioritize correct type and relative placement over quantity.
- x,y (object CENTER) must be as precise as possible — follow the three steps in
  "HOW TO PLACE" above rather than guessing a round number.
- w,h must reflect each object's REAL top-down footprint as a fraction of the room.
  Estimate true relative sizes, e.g. a large bed ≈ w 0.30–0.45 / h 0.30–0.45,
  a low table ≈ 0.18–0.28, a chair/stool ≈ 0.08–0.12. Do NOT return a constant size for everything.
- Low seating around a low table: use type low_table + floor_chair (not desk/chair).
- Place floor_chair on north/south/east/west of the low_table center when visible.
- Items against a wall: set wall to left|right|top|bottom and place x or y near that edge.
- Windows: at most ONE window object per wall; w≈0.3–0.5, h≈0.03; wall must match the actual wall.
- Skip small accessories (table lamp, laptop, plant on stool) unless they define layout.
- Include at most 2 lamps total.
- Report low-confidence objects WITH their low score; the renderer decides what to
  keep. Do not silently drop something just because you are unsure — score it honestly.
- Prefer 6–12 important objects, not 15+ overlapping items.
- Include doors/windows only if clearly visible.
